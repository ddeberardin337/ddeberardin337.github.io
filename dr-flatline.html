03 / IN DEVELOPMENT UNITY · C# · 3D HORROR

Dr. Flatline.

An independently developed first-person 3D horror game focused on environmental interaction, spatial tension, and systemic gameplay. Currently in active development.

Status In Development
Engine Unity
Language C#
01 / OVERVIEW

Building a cohesive 3D horror experience from the ground up.

Dr. Flatline is a first-person 3D horror project developed independently in Unity. The focus is on creating a tightly controlled atmosphere through level design, interaction systems, and deliberate player movement rather than relying on jump scares.

The game is being constructed modularly — each room exists as its own prefab and is assembled into a single continuous scene. This approach avoids traditional scene transitions and allows for better control over lighting, streaming, and spatial continuity.

Role Sole Developer
Focus Gameplay Systems · Level Design
Current Stage Core Systems & Environment
Platform PC (Unity)
02 / SYSTEMS IN PLACE

What’s currently functional.

Player Controller

First-person movement and collision have been implemented and tuned. The controller prioritizes weight and precision to support the slower, more deliberate pacing expected in a horror environment.

Interaction System

A basic interaction framework is in place. The player can look at and interact with objects in the world. This system currently supports doors and is structured to scale as more interactable objects are added.

Animated Doors

Fully working door system with animations. Doors open and close through the interaction system and are integrated into the modular room prefabs.

Modular Room Architecture

Each room is built as an independent prefab and introduced into a single persistent scene. This keeps the experience continuous and simplifies lighting, audio, and spatial relationships between spaces.

03 / APPROACH

How the project is being built.

Rather than jumping between multiple scenes, the game world is assembled inside one continuous scene. Rooms are developed in isolation as prefabs, then placed and connected. This method makes iteration faster and keeps the sense of space intact.

The current priority is establishing a solid foundation of player movement, interaction, and environmental structure before expanding narrative or enemy systems.

Active development.
