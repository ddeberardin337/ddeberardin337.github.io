02 / C# PROJECT C# / .NET / CONSOLE RPG

TOMES.

A text-driven RPG built around exploration, character identity, inventory-driven decisions, and branching narrative outcomes.

ROLE Solo Developer
STATUS Completed
TECHNOLOGY C# / .NET
01

OVERVIEW

TOMES is a console-based RPG developed as a final project for SGD Programming I. The assignment called for a text-based RPG. I used that foundation to create a larger interactive experience built around exploration, character creation, NPC interactions, and player-driven progression.

The entire experience is presented through text and player input. Descriptions, dialogue, inventory checks, and conditional interactions work together to create a progression system where the player's actions and collected items influence what happens next.

02

GAME STRUCTURE

Organizing the experience

TOMES uses structured collections of game data to manage its narrative and progression. Separate databases organize information about the game's items, NPCs, and navigable environments.

Lists and dictionaries provide the foundation for storing this information and connecting the different parts of the experience. Navigation is handled through player choices and destination data, rather than graphical environments or scene-based movement.

Managing player state

The player's state connects otherwise separate systems. Inventory, acquired items, character information, and progression conditions are evaluated throughout the game. This allows interactions and outcomes to change based on what the player has done.

03

GAMEPLAY SYSTEMS

Character creation

Character creation begins with a star-sign selection system. Each sign has an associated description and narrative clues. The selected sign becomes part of the player's identity as they progress through the experience.

NPC interactions

NPCs have individual dialogue and interaction logic. Their available responses and outcomes can depend on the player's inventory and progression. Some interactions involve conditional exchanges, item requirements, and choices that affect what becomes available later.

Inventory-driven progression

Items serve a purpose beyond collection. Other systems evaluate the player's inventory to determine which interactions and progression paths are available. This connects exploration, item acquisition, and character interactions into a shared gameplay loop.

Branching endings

TOMES includes multiple endings determined by the player's accumulated items and progression. The endgame evaluates the player's state and routes them to the corresponding outcome.

04

ENGINEERING

The project is written in C# and uses classes, lists, dictionaries, and static data collections to organize its systems. The central program loop coordinates player input and connects navigation, inventory, interactions, and progression.

A central challenge was ensuring that independent systems could respond to the same evolving player state. NPC interactions, item requirements, character information, and endgame conditions all needed to work together consistently as the player progressed.

Building these connections required more than implementing individual features. Each system had to account for the state established by the others, creating a cohesive experience from otherwise separate pieces of logic.

05

ENGINEERING REFLECTION

As TOMES grew in scope, some interaction logic became concentrated in a large static class. Inventory checks also relied on item-name strings, making consistency important throughout the project.

If I were extending the project today, I would look at separating interaction responsibilities into smaller components and introducing more structured item identifiers. These changes would make the systems easier to maintain and expand.

TOMES gave me practical experience with data structures, conditional logic, player state, and coordinating multiple systems within a single application. More importantly, it challenged me to think about how individual programming concepts work together to create a complete interactive experience.
