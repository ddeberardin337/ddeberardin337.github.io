DISEASED.

A collaborative Unity game developed during a time-constrained game jam. As the sole programmer, I designed and implemented the game's technical architecture, building interconnected gameplay systems and the tools the team needed to use them.

ROLE Sole Programmer
STATUS Completed
ENGINE Unity
01 / OVERVIEW

Building the foundation.

Diseased was developed by a small team working together during a game jam. While the team handled the visual direction and artwork, I was responsible for the programming, system architecture, and technical implementation.

Rather than building each feature in isolation, I structured the project around components that could communicate and work together. Player interaction, inventory, crafting, disease mechanics, NPC dialogue, and quest progression all depended on one another to support the core gameplay loop.

I also took responsibility for making those systems usable by the rest of the team, creating configured prefabs and instructional walkthroughs so visual content could be integrated without requiring teammates to work directly with the underlying code.

02 / ARCHITECTURE

Systems that work together.

I designed the project's code structure and determined how individual components would communicate. A major consideration was keeping responsibilities distributed across components while allowing different gameplay features to interact.

01

Interface-driven interaction

Implemented a shared IUsable interface that allowed different world objects to expose interaction behavior through a common contract. The player interaction controller could identify nearby usable objects, display their prompts, and invoke their interactions without needing separate interaction logic for every object type.

IUsable / PlayerInteraction
02

Data-driven inventory

Used ScriptableObjects to define item properties, categories, stack limits, and effects. Inventory state and item definitions were handled separately, allowing different items to share the same underlying inventory functionality without requiring a dedicated script for every item.

ItemData / Inventory
03

Health & disease mechanics

Separated player health management from disease behavior. The disease component handled periodic damage and suppression, while the health component remained responsible for health state, damage, and healing. Other systems could interact with these components through their public methods.

PlayerHealth / PlayerDisease
04

Recipe-driven crafting

Built crafting around reusable recipe assets defining two ingredients and a resulting item. The crafting workflow validated inventory contents, consumed the required ingredients, added the result, and refreshed the inventory interface.

CraftingRecipe / CraftingSystem
05

Quest progression

Coordinated NPC dialogue, inventory delivery, and quest progression through separate components. QuestManager tracked the current required item and controlled progression, while pickups checked whether they were currently available.

QuestManager / NPCInteraction
06

Persistent world state

Implemented persistent managers and tracked collected pickups across scene transitions. Unique pickup IDs and a HashSet allowed the world to recognize previously collected objects and prevent them from reappearing.

WorldStateManager / PersistentPlayer
03 / DEVELOPMENT TOOLING

Building beyond the runtime.

Gameplay systems needed to be accessible not only to players, but also to the people creating the game world. I built and configured the project's prefabs so teammates could place objects through a straightforward drag-and-drop workflow.

I also developed custom Unity Inspector tooling to simplify scene configuration. For example, the DoorInteraction editor allowed destination scenes to be selected and available spawn points to be discovered and configured through the Inspector.

These tools reduced the amount of manual setup required and helped keep content creation separate from the underlying implementation.

04 / TEAM WORKFLOW

Making technical systems accessible.

A working system is only useful to a team if the people building around it understand how to use it. Alongside implementation, I created private YouTube walkthroughs covering system functionality, implementation details, and proper configuration.

These walkthroughs gave teammates a reference for how gameplay features behaved and how their content should interact with the systems. Combined with preconfigured prefabs, this allowed the art team to contribute to the game world without needing to modify or understand the underlying scripts.

05 / ENGINEERING REFLECTION

Problem-solving under pressure.

Working as the sole programmer meant taking responsibility for more than individual features. I had to determine how systems should be structured, identify their dependencies, and resolve problems that emerged when separate components began interacting.

Much of the development involved learning unfamiliar implementation details, testing assumptions, and refining the architecture as the project grew. A feature could function independently and still introduce problems when connected to inventory, UI, scene management, or another gameplay system.

The experience reinforced the importance of clear component responsibilities, predictable interfaces, and designing tools around the people who will use them. It also gave me practical experience balancing technical implementation with collaboration and a fixed deadline.
